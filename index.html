Hunter Martaindale, PhD

Director of Research, ALERRT Center
Associate Research Professor, School of Criminal Justice and Criminology
Texas State University

My research focuses on policing and public safety, with particular emphasis on active shooter preparedness and response, officer stress and performance under pressure, use of force decision making, and public opinion of police. As Director of Research at the ALERRT Center, I work to translate research findings into practical guidance for law enforcement training and policy.

To follow my research lab’s ongoing work, visit the ALERRT Research website.

Active Shooter Events

Cumulative and co-occurring strains among mass public shooting perpetrators: implications for violence prevention

Schildkraut, J., Greene-Colozzi, E. A., & Martaindale, M. H. · Injury Epidemiology, 13 (2026)
This study examined the life stressors, or strains, that build up in the lives of mass public shooters, drawing on a database of 171 mass public shooting incidents and 175 perpetrators in the United States between 1999 and 2024. Rather than looking for a single triggering grievance, the researchers coded eighteen different types of strain, such as psychiatric concerns, job-related difficulties, and interpersonal conflict, and measured how often they occurred together.

Introducing Sharing Information to Stop Mass Shootings (SISMS): A short report on a behavioral and event-level dataset for the study of mass public shootings

Schildkraut, J., Greene-Colozzi, E. A., & Martaindale, M. H. · American Journal of Criminal Justice (2026)
This short report introduces Sharing Information to Stop Mass Shootings (SISMS), a new behavioral and event-level dataset built to support research on mass public shootings in the United States. The dataset currently documents 171 incidents and 175 perpetrators occurring between 1999 and 2024 for which pre-attack warning behaviors or leakage could be confirmed, drawing on public records, official documents, and triangulated open-source reporting.

Officer Stress & Performance

Box breathing and prolonged exhalation reduces markers of physiological stress reactivity in response to a virtual Trier Social Stress Test

McAllister, M. J., Martaindale, M. H., Sutton, N., & Andersen, J. P. · Comprehensive Psychoneuroendocrinology, 27 (2026)
This randomized controlled trial tested whether simple breathing techniques can blunt the body's stress response during an acutely stressful task. Sixty-six participants were assigned to normal breathing, prolonged exhalation, or box breathing before completing a virtual version of the Trier Social Stress Test, a standardized laboratory stress challenge.

Active Shooter Events

Known to the system? Criminal history, pre-attack behaviors, and firearms acquisition among mass public shooters with documented warning behaviors

Martaindale, M. H., Greene-Colozzi, E. A., & Schildkraut, J. · Journal of Criminal Justice, 105 (2026)
This study examined 175 perpetrators across 171 mass public shooting incidents in the United States between 1999 and 2024, all of whom had displayed documented pre-attack warning behaviors such as leaking their intent to others. The goal was to determine whether shooters with a prior criminal record differed from those without one in their demographics, warning signs, and how they obtained their firearms.

Public Opinion & Legitimacy

Striking or grappling? Comparing public and officers' perceptions of police use of force

*Eleuterio-da-Rocha, J., Tanksley, P. T., Martaindale, M. H., Johncox, J., & Blair, J. P. · Journal of Criminal Justice, 103 (2026)
This experiment showed short video clips of a police encounter to nearly 1,000 civilians and 744 law enforcement officers, with the clips varying in the type of force used, either a punch or a takedown, as well as the races of the officer and the suspect. Both civilians and officers consistently rated the takedown as more professional and appropriate than the punch, with takedown officers receiving excellent ratings roughly 15 to 25 percentage points more often across both groups.
